Shoot

The camera focuses and meters by itself. Each shot takes about 4–5 s.

Night

Like a phone night mode: shoots a burst, then aligns and averages the frames on the Mac. Moving things and shaky frames are left out. Works handheld; steadier is better.

Tip: set the camera to Night landscape or turn the flash off first. Frames are not kept on the card.

Long exposure

Shoots a burst and merges it on the Mac. Put the camera on something steady.

Timelapse

An intervalometer on the Mac, rendered to MP4. Shortest real interval is about 5 s.

How to connect

  1. HDMI (best): camera's mini-HDMI port (Type C) → HDMI cable → a USB HDMI capture dongle (any "UVC" one, ~$15) → Mac.
  2. AV (fallback): the yellow plug of the camera's AV cable → a composite/RCA-to-USB dongle → Mac. Standard-definition only.
  3. On the camera: Setup menu → HDMI → 720p or 1080i, and Auto off → Off (30 min max otherwise).
  4. Pick the dongle above. The browser will ask for camera permission the first time.

Over USB the stock firmware has no live view; video output is the only way. While HDMI is plugged in, the camera's own screen turns off.

Phone remote

Scan with your phone's camera. The phone must be on the same Wi-Fi.

The link carries a one-off access key; anyone with it on your Wi-Fi can use the camera until you stop the Studio. It changes every time the Studio starts.

The phone remote is off. It's opt-in, because it opens the Studio to your local network (with a random access key). Restart the Studio with:

.venv/bin/python -m studio --lan

Then open this again for the QR code. Only devices on your own network can connect.
